# Architecture: your SGO platform (a rebuild of SGO HQ's Money, Students and Books)

One SGO, no districts, **one-time gifts only, charged at checkout: no pledges, no monthly gifts, no pre-commitments.** Giving opens after January 1, 2027, when the SGO is fully set up (decided 2026-11-12). Built from `replica/recon.md` (35 screens, S-IDs) and `replica/features.csv`.

## Stack

| layer | choice | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| web | **Next.js (App Router) + TypeScript**, server actions | one app serves the admin console, the school portal, donor and family portals and the public pages; server-only data access fits a money app |
| styling | Tailwind + tokens from `/replica-design`, shadcn/ui primitives | tables, modals and forms are 80% of the UI; shadcn gives accessible ones to restyle |
| database | **Postgres on Supabase** | one database; constraint triggers keep the ledger honest; Storage and Auth in the same place |
| ORM | **Drizzle** | SQL-shaped, so the ledger queries stay readable; migrations are plain SQL (`schema.sql` is migration 0001) |
| auth | **Supabase Auth, email magic link only** | matches the original (no passwords); one-use links, 24h expiry; roles live in `memberships` |
| payments in | **Stripe**: Payment Element with ACH Direct Debit (Financial Connections) + cards; one **PaymentIntent charged at checkout** per gift. No saved payment methods, no off-session charges | donors give once, right away; Stripe holds the money, not you |
| money accounts | **Stripe Connect**: your platform account + one connected account per §25F state (payouts to that state's segregated bank account) + one general account | §25F needs a separate account per state; the original does it this way (S11). For one state that's 2 connected accounts |
| payments out | **NACHA ACH file** generated by the app, uploaded by the SGO to its own bank | the bank sends the money, so the app never holds or moves funds out; no money-transmitter problem |
| email | **Resend** + React Email templates | receipts, remittance advice, magic links, award letters; good deliverability with your own domain |
| PDFs | **@react-pdf/renderer**, rendered in a server job | receipts, year-end statements, award letters, signed agreements |
| jobs | **Inngest** | event jobs (webhook processing, receipts) and nightly reconciliation, with retries and step idempotency, without running a worker |
| files | **Supabase Storage** for logos and page images. Records go in **S3 with Object Lock in compliance mode**, in two buckets: `financial-records` (receipt PDFs, ACH files, remittance advice, W-9s, invoices, bank statements, audit packages, database archives), locked for a long renewable term with replication to a second region; `applicant-documents`, locked for 5 years | compliance-mode locks can't be lifted early by anyone, including the account owner, so files can't be deleted or overwritten by mistake or with stolen keys |
| secrets / encryption | app-level envelope encryption (libsodium) with the data key in a KMS or Supabase Vault | TINs and bank numbers are stored encrypted with last-four in clear; the database alone can't reveal them |
| hosting | **Vercel** (app, US region `iad1`, with Vercel Firewall managed rules) + Supabase (US East); files in S3 us-east-2 replicated to us-west-2. **United States only** | managed, cheap at zero users; data residency stays in the US |
| errors / logs | Sentry + Vercel logs | money bugs must be loud |

Not included on purpose: no microservices, no queue server, no separate ledger service, no browser-side database access.

## Schema

Tables: **53** (plus 14 views: account_balances, trial_balance, annual_donation_report, annual_refund_report, lifetime_transaction_ledger, ninety_ten, ninety_ten_status, receipt_year_payout, distribution_rule, returned_payments_to_spend, donor_annual_totals, vendor_bill_payability, school_scholarship_students, payments_after_leaving). See `replica/schema.sql`, tested on Postgres 16.

**Access rules:** data-layer checks. The browser never talks to the database. Every server action calls one guard (`requireRole(ctx, 'admin')`, `requireSchool(ctx, schoolId)`, `requireGuardian(ctx, householdId)`, `requireDonor(ctx, donorId)`) before its query. RLS is **on** for every table: browser roles get nothing; the server connects as **`app_runtime`**, a limited login that can read, add and update but cannot delete (except draft applications and invitations), truncate, change structure or disable triggers. Migrations run separately as the database owner; the service role key is never used by the running app.

| area | tables |
| --- | --- |
| org and access | orgs, users, memberships, schools, school_invitations, school_agreements |
| money | processor_accounts, donors, donations (attestations inline), donation_refunds, receipts, credit_caps, confirmation_counters |
| students | rounds, households, guardians, students, roster_entries, amgi_limits, applications, application_documents, income_checks, school_verifications, disqualified_persons, disqualified_matches, awards |
| payments out | payees, w9s, payee_bank_accounts, vendor_bills, delivery_proofs, family_confirmations, payment_batches, payments, payment_lines, payment_returns |
| books | ledger_accounts, journal_entries, postings, provider_events, bank_statement_lines, audit_events · views account_balances, trial_balance, ninety_ten |

Rules the **database** enforces (tested):
- Every journal entry balances, with at least 2 postings, checked at commit by a deferred constraint trigger.
- `journal_entries`, `postings` and `audit_events` are append-only. Update or delete raises an error, so corrections are reversing entries.
- `journal_entries.idempotency_key` is unique, so a webhook delivered twice posts once.
- `donations.qualified_25f` can't change once set.
- Receipts can't be deleted, and their facts can't change.
- A payee's bank details can't be approved by the person who entered them, and approval needs a call-back note. Only one live bank account per payee.
- A batch's second approver must be a different person.
- Confirmation numbers are sequential per year with no gaps from races (`next_confirmation_no`, row-locked upsert): R-2027-0001, R-2027-0002, R-2028-0001.
- Balances are computed from postings and never stored.

Rules the **data layer** enforces (need tests in `/replica-test`):
- The $1,700 per taxpayer per year cap (`credit_caps`): the sum of that donor's qualified donations for the year, counting pending ACH gifts.
- A gift received before Jan 1, 2027 is always `qualified_25f = false` (a guard only: giving opens after that date).
- An award must be paid from the §25F pool of the student's household state.
- An award is blocked while the application has an unresolved `disqualified_matches` row or no `school_verifications` row.
- Awards can't exceed received-and-unawarded §25F money for that state.
- `vendor_bills` total ≤ award left to pay.
- Batches need an approved bank account on every payee.

### Posting rules (one function per event, each with its idempotency key)

| event | debit | credit | key |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gift succeeds (Stripe) | asset:stripe:25f:ST (gross) | income:contributions:25f:ST | `<event_id>:gift` |
| its Stripe fee | expense:fees:processing | asset:stripe:25f:ST | `fee:<balance_transaction_id>` (amount from the balance transaction, never computed; it can arrive with `payment_intent.succeeded` or a later `charge.updated`) |
| offline check/wire | asset:clearing:deposits | income:contributions:… | `offline:<donation_id>` |
| Stripe payout to bank | asset:bank:25f:ST | asset:stripe:25f:ST | `<payout_event_id>` |
| refund, ACH return, lost dispute | income:contribution_refunds:ST (+ dispute fee to expense:fees) | asset:stripe:25f:ST | `refund:<donation_refund_id>`; source_type `donation_refund`, linked to the original gift |
| award approved | expense:scholarships:ST | liability:awards_payable:ST | `award:<id>` |
| award cancelled | reversing entry | | `award:<id>:cancel` |
| batch marked sent | liability:awards_payable:ST | asset:clearing:disbursements | `batch:<id>:sent` |
| batch settled (statement match) | asset:clearing:disbursements | asset:bank:25f:ST | `batch:<id>:settled` |
| payment returned by bank, or a school/vendor sends money back | asset:bank:25f:ST | income:returned_payments:ST | `return:<payment_return_id>`; a `payment_returns` row with spend-by = Dec 31 of the next year |
| returned money put back on its award | expense:scholarships:ST | liability:awards_payable:ST | `return:<id>:to_award`; counts as scholarship spending again |

The **90/10** view reads contributions (gross), refunds, scholarships and fees plus admin straight from these postings, per calendar year, so the test can be run gross or net of refunds once your CPA picks the basis. The **reversal window** is computed as succeeded gifts younger than 120 days (card) or 60 days (ACH), from `donations.succeeded_at`.

**The Committee member role**
- **Recommends; owner or admin decides.** Each member records approve / deny / abstain, a suggested amount (required for approve), a 1–5 score and a comment. The owner or admin sees every recommendation beside the income check and makes the decision. Only an owner or admin can create an award (database-enforced).
- **Optional minimum:** Settings › Scholarships › "recommendations needed before an award" (0–9, default 0). With 2, an award is refused until two members have recommended (database-enforced).
- **Sees everything on an application, including documents.** Every document opened is logged with member and time.
- **Automatically a disqualified person.** Giving someone the role adds them to the §25F disqualified-persons list ("selection committee"), so the name-match check flags any application from them or their family. Add their family members in Settings › Scholarships as usual.
- **Conflicts of interest.** A member must recuse (with a reason, kept permanently) when related to the family or connected to the school. The database refuses a recommendation from a member who is staff at the student's school, or whose own or family name matches the application. The app also hides recused applications from that member's queue.
- **Locked after the decision.** Recommendations can be edited until the application is decided, then never; they are never deleted. They are part of the decision record auditors see.
- **No money access.** Members never see donations, payments, bank details, the ledger or settings.
- **Hears about new work by email:** a daily digest by default (only on days something new is waiting), or one email per application, or off; their choice.
- **Granted by an owner**, removable any time (access ends immediately; their past recommendations and their disqualified-person entry stay).

**The Auditor role, specifically**
- **Read-only, enforced twice:** every server action that changes anything refuses the auditor role, and the auditor's session gets no write actions in the UI at all.
- **Time-limited:** an owner grants access with an end date, at most one year (the database refuses longer or open-ended). Default 90 days, renewable by an owner. Access ends automatically at the end date, or right away when an owner revokes it.
- **Only an owner can grant it**, never an admin, and an owner can't grant it to themselves (database-enforced).
- **Family documents are off by default.** Most audits need the decision and its numbers (income, limit, % of limit), which the auditor always sees. If the audit needs the documents themselves, the owner switches them on for that auditor; each document opened is logged.
- **Sensitive numbers stay masked:** TINs and bank account numbers show last 4 digits only, in screens and exports.
- **Everything logged:** sign-in, every page viewed and every download (which year, which file) goes to the audit log, so the SGO can show exactly what was accessed.
- **Lands on Compliance** after sign-in, with the year picker and the audit package one click away.
- Records the firm name (e.g. "Smith & Co CPAs") and who granted the access.

**No donor earmarking** is enforced by design: a donor can designate a school or "greatest need", and the data model has no field that could name a student.

Reports and term confirmations are permanent; an owner or admin can record either on the school's behalf (by phone, email or letter, with a note), as with enrollment confirmation.

## Paying schools, vendors and providers: proof of delivery (decided 2026-11-13)

**ACH, fast and low-cost.** Payments go in batches (one ACH payment per payee, a line per student, remittance advice by email). The app generates a NACHA file and the SGO's own bank sends it, so the only cost is the bank's ACH fee and the app holds no money. Standard ACH usually settles in 1–2 business days; the batch's payment date can be set to the same day for **same-day ACH** if the SGO's bank offers it.

**Nothing is paid until it is proven.** The database refuses to put a line into a payment batch until its proof is recorded (tested):

| payee | required before payment |
| --- | --- |
| **School** | the school has confirmed enrollment (in its portal, or recorded on its behalf), **and** confirmed the student is **still enrolled for that term**, **and** the school's tuition statement or invoice **for that term** is on file, **and** no withdrawal, transfer or graduation has been reported. Each school line names its term (e.g. "2027-28 Fall"); a Fall statement doesn't cover Spring |
| **Vendor or provider** (tutor, therapist, supplier, technology) | an **invoice** **and** proof of delivery: a delivery receipt, a session log, or a signed confirmation, with the service dates |
| **…plus, if the SGO turns it on** (Settings › Payments › "Ask families to confirm delivery") | the parent's one-click confirmation by email: "Did Leo receive these 24 tutoring sessions?" Yes or "No, here's what happened." A disputed answer blocks payment until an owner or admin records how it was resolved; the family's answer is never changed |

Bills without full proof sit in a new **Awaiting proof** tab (Payments), showing exactly what's missing; they move to **Ready to pay** by themselves once it's in. Schools can upload their term statement in the school portal; vendors' documents are uploaded by staff (or sent by the vendor and attached). Every proof file goes into the locked financial-records bucket and is kept permanently with the payment.

## Donor tax reporting and annual certification (decided 2026-11-13)

**January 31: year-end statements (must).** One statement per donor per tax year: donor number, name and address, the SGO's EIN, number of gifts, §25F-designated total, other gifts, and any refunds of designated gifts known at the time. Generated from `donor_annual_totals`, saved as a permanent snapshot (`year_end_statements`) with its PDF, emailed, and downloadable in the donor portal. If something changes later (for example a refund in February), a **corrected statement** is issued that points to the one it replaces and states why; the original is kept.

**February 28: IRS donor report (must).** One report per tax year listing each donor's number, name, address and §25F-designated amount (and refunds of it), generated as a permanent snapshot (`irs_donor_reports` + `irs_donor_report_lines`) and a file to upload to the IRS SGO portal. Someone then records the submission: who, when and the portal's confirmation number (required). Figures can't change after generation; a correction is a new report that points to the original. **The file layout is a placeholder (`placeholder-v0`)** until the IRS publishes it; confirm the deadlines with your CPA.

**Payout progress by receipt year (must).** `receipt_year_payout`: for each state and each year's receipts, the 90% that must be paid out, how much has been paid toward it, what's still to pay, the deadline (Dec 31 of the next year) and status (met / to pay / overdue). Payments are applied to the oldest year first. Shown on the Compliance page and, when a deadline is within 90 days, in the Overview banner.

**Annual certification data pack (must).** Once a year, per state: a frozen snapshot of the year's figures (receipts, scholarships, 90% test on both bases, distribution rule, payout progress, donors, refunds, returned payments, open reviews) with a PDF. **Only an owner can certify it**, typing a certification statement; after that it can't change (a superseding pack is created if needed). Used for the state's annual certification and kept permanently.

## Refunds and the three financial views (decided 2026-11-12)

A refund never touches the original gift. Example: a $1,700 gift in June 2027, $500 refunded in March 2028.

1. The 2027 donation (R-2027-0001) and its receipt stay exactly as they were.
2. The refund is its own permanent transaction in `donation_refunds`: kind (refund, ACH return, or lost dispute), amount, the date the money went back, **counts_in_year = 2028**, the original tax year (2027), reason, and Stripe ids.
3. It is linked to the original by `donation_id` and appears with it in every report.
4. It gets its own sequential number (**RF-2028-0001**) and a **refund receipt** PDF emailed to the donor. Both receipts are kept permanently.
5. **Every refund of a §25F gift is flagged for tax-compliance review**: priority `cross_year` when the refund year is later than the gift's tax year, otherwise `normal`. Review status goes open → donor notified / no action needed / resolved, closed only with a reviewer and a note.

The database enforces all of it (tested): refunds can't exceed the gift (partial refunds allowed), §25F refunds can't skip the flag or get the wrong priority, refund facts can't change, refunds can't be deleted, and a review can't close without who and why.

| view | answers | source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Annual Donation Report** | donations received in each calendar year: count, received, §25F vs general, fees, donors | `annual_donation_report` (from donations; never changes after the year) |
| **Annual Refund Report** | refunds actually paid in each calendar year: count, total, by kind, §25F share, cross-year count, reviews still open | `annual_refund_report` (from donation_refunds) |

## Records retention (decided 2026-11-12)

### Financial records: permanent

**Receipts, payment records, the books and ledgers, and every supporting financial document are kept permanently and are never deleted or destroyed.** They stay online and searchable for audit, compliance and financial transparency.

| record | where | protection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Donors, donations (amount, fee, method, designation, attestations), receipts and receipt PDFs | `donors`, `donations`, `receipts` + financial-records bucket | no delete or truncate; a completed gift's amount, donor, method, designation, state, tax year and confirmation number can't change; a recorded fee can't change; receipt facts and PDFs can't change. Refunds, returns and disputes are new status + reversing journal entries |
| Awards | `awards` | no delete or truncate; cancel with a reversing entry |
| School disbursements: batches, payments, per-student lines, ACH files as sent, remittance advice | payment tables + bucket | no delete or truncate (a draft batch is cancelled, not deleted); a payment's amount, payee and bank account can't change once its batch is approved |
| Vendor bills and invoices, W-9s, bank-detail history, bank statement lines | their tables + bucket | no delete or truncate; replaced or superseded, never removed |
| The books: chart of accounts, journal entries, postings, processor events, confirmation counters, cap table | ledger tables | append-only (no update, delete or truncate); corrections are reversing entries |
| Supporting decision records: income checks, school verifications, disqualified-person resolutions, school agreements | their tables | no delete or truncate |
| Audit log | `audit_events` | append-only, permanent |

All of this is enforced by **database triggers**, so it holds whichever code path or admin tries it. Tested: deleting or truncating donations, donors, postings or batches is refused; editing a completed gift's amount or recorded fee is refused; refunds and returns still work.

### Applicant documents: five years

Family and applicant files (W-2s, tax returns, pay stubs, benefit letters, foster placement, other-income proof) are kept at least **5 years** from the decision, or from the round close if never decided. After that the monthly `retention-sweep` deletes the files and logs it. The application row and its income check stay permanently, because they back the award.

The application's privacy note says documents are kept five years after the decision. The original says they're deleted at decision; yours must not.

### Staying accessible

- **Auditor role:** a read-only, time-limited login for your CPA or an examiner (see Roles and permissions).
- **Audit package for any year**, any time: nothing is archived out of reach. Old years stay in the live database (an SGO's volume is small).
- **Backups:** Supabase point-in-time recovery; a nightly encrypted dump to the financial-records bucket, with monthly dumps kept permanently.
- **If you ever leave the platform or the host:** a full export (CSV + PDFs + ledger) is a documented job, so the records outlive the software.

## The parts that bite

- **Time zones:** tax year = the year of the gift in the SGO's time zone (America/Chicago), not UTC. An ACH gift made Dec 30 that clears Jan 3 needs a decided rule (recommended: the date the donor gave; confirm with your CPA). Pay-by dates are `date`.
- **Idempotency:** Stripe webhooks arrive twice and out of order. Unique `stripe_event_id`, unique `idempotency_key` on journal entries, and a Stripe idempotency key on every PaymentIntent create (the checkout id + gift number), so a double-clicked Give button charges once.
- **ACH is slow and can bounce:** a bank gift is `processing` for about 4 business days. Book it only on `succeeded`, and only then issue the receipt. A later return or dispute reverses it. The award screens warn while money is inside the 60/120-day window.
- **Races:** two admins awarding at once could overspend a state pool. `decideApplication` and `batchAward` take a `pg_advisory_xact_lock` on (org, state) and re-check the available balance inside the transaction. The cap check at checkout locks the donor row the same way. Confirmation numbers come from a row-locked counter.
- **Fees are facts, not formulas:** record Stripe's actual fee from the balance transaction. Don't compute 2.9%. The donor's "cover the fee" add-on is an estimate shown at checkout and stored separately (`fee_covered_cents`).
- **Sensitive data:** TINs and bank numbers are encrypted in the app, with last-four in clear. Tax documents are encrypted per file before upload, sit in a locked, private bucket and are kept 5 years (see Records retention and Security). The audit log records every view of a full TIN, bank number or tax document. Admins need MFA, and Supabase Auth supports TOTP; require it for owner and admin.
- **Rate limits:** magic-link and eligibility endpoints are rate-limited by IP and email (Upstash). The give endpoint needs a bot check (Turnstile) because card testing is a real risk.
- **File limits:** 10 MB per document. JPEG, PNG, HEIC and PDF only, checked server-side by content type and size before the signed URL is issued.
- **Email deliverability:** send from your own domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set. Receipts are legal records, so log `emailed_at` and allow a resend.
- **Search:** Postgres `ilike` on name and email with trigram indexes is enough at one SGO's scale.
- **Realtime / offline:** not needed. Refresh after actions.
- **Multi-tenancy:** single SGO, but `org_id` is on top-level tables, so going multi-SGO later means adding a tenant guard, not a schema rewrite.
- **Deletion requests:** a family or donor asking to be deleted can't override retention: financial records are permanent and applicant documents are kept 5 years. Answer with what is kept, why and until when; delete marketing data (emails, roster contacts) right away.
- **Rules may change:** the $1,700 cap, the 300% AMGI test, the 90/10 split and the reversal windows are configuration (`credit_caps`, `amgi_limits`, org settings), not constants, because Treasury's final regulations aren't out.
